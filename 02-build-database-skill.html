PART II • STEP 1

Start With the Problem

Every good Skill starts from a real, annoying, repetitive job — stated plainly.

“I want to create a database to track my client interactions and identify churn risk and upsell opportunities.”

Why state it out loud

If you can't say the job in one sentence, the prompt won't be any clearer. The sentence above is the whole brief.

Why this one hurts

The information already exists — scattered across call recaps, usage data, notes and your own memory. Nobody has time to reconcile it weekly.

Your version

Swap in the repetitive job that eats your week. The rest of this page works the same way regardless of what you put in that sentence.

PART II • STEP 2

Answer Four Questions First

Do this before you open Cowork. Your answers become the content of the prompt — and later, the content of the Skill.

STEP 01

Locate your sources

List every place this data already lives — call-analysis emails, Gainsight, your inbox, Teams, your own notes. Cowork can only use what you can point it to.

In practice: Gong call-analysis emails, Gainsight notes, your renewal tracker.
STEP 02

Name the gaps

Be explicit about what no source covers today — verbal commitments, sentiment, competitor mentions. Gaps are the fields you still fill in by hand.

In practice: the promise made on the call that never reached Gainsight.
STEP 03

Define your signals

Write down what churn risk and upsell actually look like in your data. This one decides your columns — you can't build the right table without it.

In practice: activation under 60% = risk; over 90% and rising = upsell.
STEP 04

Define the update loop

Decide what triggers a refresh and how often — after every call, weekly, or monthly. You won't act on this today: it's the last thing you do, and it's what Part 3 turns into a scheduled task.

In practice: a Monday 8am sweep of last week's calls and new emails.

The one people skip: Step 03. Skip it and you get a database that describes your accounts but never tells you to act.

Why this order: the four questions run in the same sequence as the session — build (01), refine (02), skill (03), schedule (04). Answer them top to bottom and you've written your prompt without realising it.

PART II • BUILD A

Gong Call Recordings Database

The qualitative signal — what your customers actually said. Build it, refine it until it's right, then ask it a question.

Run these in the same chat. Notice the build prompt already states what you want to detect — that's what decides the columns.

STEP 1: Build it

Say what you're trying to spot up front, so the table is designed to answer that question.

I want a database that helps me spot churn risk and upsell opportunities from what customers say on calls.

Search my email for Gong call analysis emails from the last 90 days.

Create a spreadsheet in my OneDrive called Gong Call Log with one row per call and these columns: date, account, attendees, call purpose, key topics discussed, customer sentiment, objections raised, and agreed next steps.

So I can flag risk later, also capture whether each call mentioned: pricing pressure, a champion or key stakeholder leaving, or hiring slowing down.

Fill it in from those emails. If something isn't mentioned in the email, leave it blank rather than guessing.
STEP 2: REFINE IT — THIS IS WHERE THE TIME GOES

You will not get it right first time, and that's the normal experience rather than a failure. Open the spreadsheet, find what's wrong, and say so in plain language. Real corrections sound like:

  • The account column is picking up the sender's company, not the customer. Use the company in the subject line instead.
  • Split ‘agreed next steps’ into two columns: what I owe them, and what they owe me.
  • Ignore internal calls — only include calls where someone outside LinkedIn was on the invite.
  • ‘Sentiment’ is too vague. Use positive, neutral or at risk, and tell me which phrase made you choose it.

Keep going until you'd trust the table in front of your manager. Every correction you make here gets baked into the Skill later — so this step is doing double duty.

STEP 3: Ask it the question

This proves the table works. It's also the prompt you'll reuse every week.

Read Gong Call Log and flag any account whose last two calls mention pricing pressure, a champion leaving, or hiring on pause.

Show each flag with the quote from the call that triggered it, so I can judge it myself rather than taking your word for it.

Then list any account I haven't had a call with in over 60 days.
PART II • BUILD B

Product Health Database

The quantitative signal — what the product data shows. Same three beats: build, refine, then ask.

The pattern is deliberately identical to Build A. That repeatability is the actual lesson — the subject changes, the shape doesn't.

STEP 1: Build it

Your thresholds from Step 04 of the design method belong right here, in the build.

I want a database that helps me spot churn risk and upsell opportunities from how my accounts are actually using the product.

Create a spreadsheet in my OneDrive called Product Health with one row per account and these columns: contract seats, seats activated, activation rate, weekly active recruiters, InMail acceptance rate, Hiring Assistant adoption, last QBR date, renewal date, and open RTA.

Populate it from my most recent usage export. Leave any field blank that the export doesn't cover — don't estimate.

Show activation rate as a percentage of contract seats, since that's the number I'll be judging risk on.
STEP 2: REFINE IT — THIS IS WHERE THE TIME GOES

Usage exports are messy and yours won't match this template exactly. Open the spreadsheet, find what's off, and correct it conversationally. Real corrections sound like:

  • Drop the InMail column — we don't sell that to these accounts.
  • Add a column for days until renewal, calculated from today's date.
  • Some accounts appear twice under slightly different names. Merge them and keep the higher seat count.
  • Sort it by open RTA, highest first, so the accounts that matter most are at the top.

Keep going until you'd trust the table in front of your manager. Every correction you make here gets baked into the Skill later — so this step is doing double duty.

STEP 3: Ask it the question

Now the thresholds earn their keep. Change the numbers to ones you'd actually defend.

Read Product Health and flag churn risk where activation is under 60%, or where weekly active recruiters have fallen two months running.

Flag upsell opportunity where activation is above 90% and Hiring Assistant adoption is rising.

Sort by open RTA, highest first, and tell me which three accounts I should act on this week and why.

60% and 90% are examples, not standards. Use whatever you'd defend in a pipeline review. The value isn't in the number — it's in having written the rule down at all.

PART II • STEP 3

Store It Somewhere Cowork Can Find It

Storing your database in a consistent, predictable location is what unlocks the automation later. Get this wrong and every prompt starts with a search.

One home, always

Keep it in your OneDrive in the same folder every time. If Cowork has to hunt for the file, a scheduled run has nothing reliable to open.

Name it and reuse the name

Refer to it by exactly the same name in every prompt. “Gong Call Log” every time — not “my call log” one week and “the call tracker” the next.

One file, many questions

Resist making a new sheet per question. One well-structured table answers churn, upsell and QBR prep — three tables drift apart within a month.

The test: if you can tell a colleague where the file is and what it's called in one sentence, Cowork can find it too.

PART II • THE PAYOFF

Now Turn It Into a Skill

Do this last — only once your database returns what you actually want. A Skill locks in a working process, so saving a half-right one early just means getting it half-right forever.

Run this in the same chat where you refined your table, so Cowork can see what worked and what you corrected along the way.

SAVE THE PROCESS

Replace the bracketed parts with whichever database you built.

Create a skill called [your-skill-name] from what we just built in this chat.

It should capture:
- Where my data comes from: [your sources]
- The database it maintains: [file name and location]
- The columns it fills in and how it decides each one
- How often it should refresh: [your cadence]
- What counts as churn risk: [your rule]
- What counts as an upsell opportunity: [your rule]
- The corrections I made during this chat, so it doesn't repeat those mistakes

Write it so that next time I only have to say "[the short phrase you'd naturally type]" and it runs the whole process.

THEN TEST IT IN A NEW CHAT

A fresh chat is the only real test — that's the entire point of a Skill.

Using my [your-skill-name] skill, refresh my database and show me what changed since the last run.

Why this order matters: a Skill is a recipe. Write the recipe once the dish tastes right — not while you're still adjusting the seasoning.

PART II • RECAP

What to Take Away

Four things worth remembering when you try this on your own accounts.

Design, then prompt

The four questions do the hard work. A prompt written after answering them is short; one written before them is a guess.

Refining is the work

The first output is a draft. The value comes from the corrections you make next — and those corrections become the Skill.

Write the rule down

If you can't state what churn risk looks like in your data, Cowork can't either. Gut feel doesn't survive a scheduled run.

Automate last

Build, refine, Skill it, then schedule it. Automating something you haven't validated just makes it wrong every week.

WHAT PART 3 DOES WITH THIS

Once your database is right and saved as a Skill, this is the wording that becomes a scheduled task — so it runs without you. Fill in the brackets for whichever build you did.

Every [day and time], check for new [your source] since the last run and add them to [your database] as new rows.

Never add the same record twice — check [the field that identifies a duplicate] before adding.

Then tell me how many rows were added, and flag anything that now meets my churn risk or upsell rules.
